I am a final-semester MASc student in Systems Design Engineering at the University of Waterloo, where I work in the Vision & Image Processing (VIP) Lab with Prof. John S. Zelek. My research is on physics-grounded neural reconstruction — bringing physical laws into 3D Gaussian Splatting and neural rendering so that reconstructed scenes are not just photorealistic but physically usable. I work across differentiable simulation (rigid-body and the Material Point Method), reinforcement learning for inverse problems, and real-time GPU rendering, with the broader goal of closing the real‑to‑sim‑to‑real loop for embodied and physical AI.

Before Waterloo, I earned my BASc in Computer Engineering from the University of Toronto. I have also worked in industry as a digital logic design intern at Intel and as a full-stack developer building a national education platform.

I am currently on the market for Research Scientist / Research Engineer roles in computer vision, graphics, and robotics, starting in September 2026.

PersistGS: Differentiable Physics for Object Permanence in 4D Gaussian Splatting

Adrian Ramlal, John S. Zelek

CVPR 2026 Generative 3D Reconstruction Workshop · pp. 4687–4696 Oral

Differentiable rigid-body simulation inside 4D Gaussian Splatting keeps objects consistent through full occlusion: +2.46 dB PSNR over kinematic baselines, within 0.19 dB of the ground-truth upper bound, with a centroid-silhouette loss that cuts trajectory error 40%.

Also presented at the ICRA 2026 Real2Sim/Sim2Real Workshop (non-archival).

Latent Space Reinforcement Learning for Inverse Material Estimation in Food Fracture Simulation

Adrian Ramlal, Yuhao Chen, John S. Zelek

CVPR 2026 MetaFood Workshop · pp. 9573–9581 Poster

A goal-conditioned PPO policy in a normalizing-flow latent space recovers material parameters of a non-differentiable MPM fracture simulator in ~8 evaluations (~10 ms) — 625× more efficient than evolutionary search.
